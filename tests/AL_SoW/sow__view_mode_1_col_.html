Learning Objectives


By the end of the sub-unit, students should be able to:

• Perform essential algebraic manipulations, such as:- Brackets, Like Terms, Factorising etc

• Use and manipulate Surds, including rationalising the denominator

• Understand and use the Laws of Indices for all rational exponents

1a Indices & Surds

 Print

4h

Specification references

2.1

Understand and use the laws of indices for all rational exponents

2.2

Use and manipulate surds, including rationalising the denominator

Teaching points


A recap of skills taught at GCSE Higher Tier (9-1)


Emphasise that in many cases, only a fraction or surd can express the exact answer, so that it is important to be able to calculate with surds.


Ensure pupils understand that root a plus root b is not equal to root a plus b.


Most students understand the skills needed to complete these calculations but make basic errors with arithmetic leading to incorrect solutions.


Questions involving squares e.g. 〖(2√3)〗^2 will need practice.


Students should be exposed to lots of simplifying questions involving fractions as this is where most marks are lost in the exam.


Recap the difference of two squares (x + y)(x - y) and link this to  , hence choice of term to rationalise the denominator.

Opportunities for reasoning/problem solving


Examples involving areas of shapes with ‘surd’ length sides. Exact solutions for Pythagoras questions.

Common misconceptions/Examiner report quotes


Many candidates misinterpreted  [(a√b)]^2 as [(a+ √b)]^2


[(√2)]^2 is often evaluated as 4 instead of 2.


Most candidates were familiar with the technique for rationalising the denominator and, although there were some who made slips when multiplying out the two brackets in the numerator, most obtained the correct answer in the given form. Once again, some candidates cancelled incorrectly and thus did not obtain the correct final answer. Poor basic arithmetic e.g. 1x1=2 and 20 – 2 =16 leads to incorrect solutions, evidence of not checking work.


Most students then simplified their expression to (2√2)/(7√2). A surprisingly large number of students left this as their final answer or presented the final answer as ("2" √("2" ))/"7" .

Notes

Make use of matching activities (e.g. Tarsia Puzzles)
