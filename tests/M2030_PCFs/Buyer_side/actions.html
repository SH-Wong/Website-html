Implement a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) to reduce scope 2 emissions

clear

Tips & advice


A PPA can take many forms, depending on whether the market is regulated or not.

There are two main types of off-site PPA to choose from (definitions sourced from rmi.org):

Physical PPA—Within a physical PPA contract, the corporate buyer takes ownership of the electrons produced by the renewable energy project. This transaction places responsibility on the buyer for monetizing/selling the electrons, which is typically achieved by selling them into the wholesale electricity market. Depending on the contract structure, the buyer could also pay for transmission charges.


Virtual PPA—Within a VPPA contract, the corporate buyer does not own and is not responsible for the physical electrons generated by the project. The VPPA is purely a financial transaction, exchanging a fixed-price cash flow for a variable-priced cash flow and renewable energy certificates (RECs).


Because the VPPA is purely financial, the buyer still needs to meet its electricity load through traditional channels—therefore, the VPPA means the buyer’s relationship with its utility at the retail level remains unchanged. Because VPPAs are purely financial transactions, they allow buyers that have highly distributed electricity load—or load in regulated markets—to meet their renewable energy goals quickly and efficiently.


A PPA’s contract price or VPPA’s 'strike price' represents the fixed cost to the buyer per unit of electricity generated.


The revenue that the buyer can generate by re-selling the electricity they own from their PPA/VPPA is subject to market rates, and therefore is likely to be volatile. The lifetime of the PPA/VPPA is likely to belong (>10 years) and therefore gains/losses can vary as the market changes.


Some buyers are using PPAs/VPPAs to hedge against future prices, while others are just looking to break even while receiving the EACs their PPA/VPPA allows them to claim.


Considerations and tips when considering a PPA


⦁ Seek expert advice. These are long term commitments.


⦁ The technology used: Type of technology directly impacts the cost, impact to the environment and security of supply. It is however important to consider the government’s policy on the appropriate overall energy mix for the country’s electricity grid.


⦁ Location: For VPPAs, location is important due to restrictions surrounding policy jurisdiction when purchasing EACs.


To model and track the potential impact of this change in Bee:


Check both ‘electricity (standard)’ and ‘electricity (renewable purchased)’ is enabled on your settings page.


If you are making the switch partway through your reporting year, estimate the amount of EACs from your PPA that you’ll retire for the rest of the reporting year.


Add this amount to the ‘electricity (standard)’ saving box and add the negative equivalent value of ‘electricity (renewable)’.


Keep ‘estimate my CO2’ ticked.


This will estimate the CO2 saving of the change while keeping your energy consumption consistent in your reports.


At the yearend for reporting annual measurements, simply calculate your total electricity use. For electricity, you have retired EACs against, record this as ‘electricity (renewable purchased), and the balance as ‘electricity (standard)’

About this action


A Power Purchase Agreement (PPA), also referred to as an offtake agreement, is a contract that guides the sale and purchase of power between an electricity seller/producer and a buyer.


PPAs are used to provide a level of financial certainty for Independent Power Producers (IPPs), at times critical for their initial investment funding, and are often seen as a key mechanism to encourage ongoing investment in renewable energy generation.


A PPA can play an important role in a carbon reduction strategy, as they allow the buyer to claim the environmental credits (Energy Attributable Certificates (EACs)) and manage medium to long term energy prices.


PPAs are seen as actively helping to develop renewable energy infrastructure by helping new projects get funding. They are therefore preferable to the mass purchase of EACs to reduce Scope 2 emissions (though both are valid accounting mechanisms).
