• Not started : Buyer selected, no data submitted

• A : Buyer selected, some data submitted

• G : Buyer selected, all required data submitted.

Org level

• Inactive : None of the facilities have buyer selected : (All facilities = Inactive)

• Not started : 1+ facility have buyer selected, no data submitted by those facilities : (1+ facility not started, none A or G)

• A : 1+ facility have buyer selected, some/all data submitted by some facilities : (1+ facility A or G, but not all are G)

• G : 1+ facility have buyer selected, all data submitted by all facilities : (All facilities are G)

Carlsberg Group announces launch of paper-based bottles with a revolutionary plant-based lining!

Packaging

grain

Context

Our recent packaging breakthrough is integral to Carlsberg’s “Together Towards ZERO” sustainability programme. By 2030, we are aiming to achieve zero CO₂ in our breweries and to reduce value chain CO₂ emissions by 30%. We’ve paved the way for this latest innovation with our previous achievements, including our innovative ‘Snap Pack’, which uses bonding technology to secure cans instead of plastic rings. Carlsberg’s vision for the Fibre Bottle is to provide consumers with a low-carbon alternative to traditional beer bottles and cans.


100% Bio-based

The latest ‘Generation 2.0’ design has been three years in the making – we released our prototypes of the first recyclable and bio-based bottles back in 2019. This inspired the creation of Paboco, the Paper Bottle Company - a joint project between packaging company BillerudKorsnäs and plastic bottle manufacturer Alpla. Working alongside Paboco and partner Avantium, who developed the plant-based polyethylene furanoate (PEF) polymer lining, Carlsberg has made a breakthrough with our latest bottle, which consists of a wood fiber shell and the PEF lining made entirely from natural raw materials. This makes the bottle 100% bio-based (excluding the cap) and fully recyclable. Previous versions of the fiber bottle had consisted of only 57% paper and a PE barrier.



          "It's not only better for the environment, but we are confident that the new bottles protect the fizziness and taste of the beer better than conventional plastic bottles, and the shell’s insulative properties help to keep the beer cooler for longer when compared with glass bottles or cans"



The upcoming trial will be the biggest we’ve conducted yet, distributing eight thousand bottles across eight markets in Europe including the UK, France, Germany, and Poland. As a result of this trial, we will gain customer and consumer feedback on the bottles, which we will utilize to guide the next iteration of our design.


The design is being continuously improved to reduce the carbon footprint of the Fibre Bottle as far as possible, whilst guaranteeing the same product quality and supporting the scaling-up of PEF production and its new possibilities. Tracking and measuring both the environmental impact and the quality of the beer inside is essential to know we are making improvements.


This project has not been without its challenges, Stéphane Munch, our vice president for group development, said:


“Identifying and producing PEF as a competent functional barrier for beer has been one of our greatest challenges, so getting good test results, collaborating with suppliers, and seeing the bottles being filled on the line is a great achievement.”


This current phase of testing the bottles will place them into the hands of local consumers, customers, and other stakeholders through select festivals and flagship events, as well as targeted product samplings. Testing at this scale will give Carlsberg the opportunity to gather feedback on people’s experiences of the product, which will inform the next generation of design.


Our partner Avantium has developed the bio-based polymer solution (PEF), and The Fibre Bottle has been developed together with our other partners in the Paper Bottle Community, including the Paper Bottle Company (Paboco), Coca Cola in Europe, P&G, L’Oreal, The Absolut Company, and others. Cross-industry collaboration and complementary competencies have been and will continue to be, key to achieving innovation milestones like the Fibre Bottle and its variations to suit our partners’ different products (from our beer to Coca-Cola’s soft drinks, L’Oreal’s cosmetics, P&G´s home goods and more).


There’s also a challenge that presents itself in the form of scalability. Currently, the technology to make the fiber bottle is not at the point where large-scale commercial introduction is feasible. However, this rollout will be key in making it a reality.


Future steps!

Before a commercial rollout of the new fiber bottle, a recycling assessment will be conducted to identify the best recycling pathway for the bottles. Options could include deposit return schemes, where the material is recycled through paper and cardboard recycling streams. We will be working on this as we progress towards generation 3.0.


In the life cycle assessment (LCA), Generation 2.0 performs better than single-use glass bottles, but we have bigger ambitions still for our next design, Generation 3.0. Our ambition is for the fiber bottle to achieve up to 80% fewer emissions than the current single-use glass bottles – this is supported by our current projections. Therefore, the carbon footprint attached to creating a single glass bottle will be equal to that of creating five fiber bottles. Our ultimate aim is for the fiber bottle to have the same carbon footprint as the refillable glass bottle, currently the best-performing primary packaging when it is collected and reused appropriately.
