PRODUCT CARBON FOOTPRINT BUILDER

UX research and design Interaction design

Upcoming global legislations for organizations to report the environment impact of imported goods (in particular CBAM), means that corporations and manufacturers are faced with new needs to calculate Product Carbon Footprints (PCFs) for individual products that are traded across those regions. This presented a new opportunity for Manufacture 2030 (M2030) to help their users with tools to build PCFs and satisfy reporting demands.

PROBLEM

While in recent years, understanding of the importance of sustainability accounting has increased, manufacturing businesses with dedicated resources and matured knowledge of sustainability accounting are still in the minority in the global supply chain. Particularly so in term of PCFs, as a relatively new reporting demand.

So to help these manufacturers with varying maturity in sustainability knowledge respond to incoming legislations and customer demands, we asked: Can we enable users to calculate PCFs without extensive sustainability knowledge?

RESEARCH

  • Learn from the experts: What are PCFs, and how are they calculated?
  • Learn from the competitors: What is is working well (or not)?
  • Learn from the users: What makes a valuable PCF feature? (Survey & persona)

Learning summary

From the various research we derived:

  • A highly guided design that assumes minimal specialist knowledge and language would be ideal.As a majority of M2030 users have indicated low maturity and knowledge on PCFs.
  • Some automations is essential but should be balanced with a good level of accuracy and specificity.This is important to encourage adoption, as manufacturer indicated that ‘resources’ is the 2nd largest limiting factor in building PCFs; but without a good level of accuracy, the value of the results would be limited
  • Initially align with a non-sector specific PCF methodology framework (PACT).As M2030’s audience operates across many different sectors, it would make senses to adopt a methodology that caters for the widest possible range of industries. Additional industry level specificities can then be further developed.

Learn from the experts

As PCFs is a complicated sustainability subject, we worked with sustainability experts to gather some basic understanding of the challenge and requirements in PCF before jumping into the design:

Some key learnings:

  • Product Carbon footprint (PCF) is an environmental impact accounting method that works alongside more established methods such as Lifecycle assessment (LCA) and corporate/facility-level accounting.
  • Benefit of PCFs is that it is a middle ground between the 2 aforementioned methods.
    • It allows organizations to calculate the emission intensity of individual products.
    • It require much less data compared to LCAs. (How every this still means substantial effort are needed.)
  • There are many different standards for how PCFs should be calculated. Some caters more towards specific industries, for example: Catena-X (automotive) and TFS (Chemical), while others are more general (PACT)

How are they calculated?

We also gained a basic technical understanding of how PCFs are calculated. As with other environmental accounting methods, PCF calculations are fundamentally based on:

  1. The amount of inputs that goes into the product manufacturing process, including:
    1. Materials
    2. Packaging
    3. Transportation
    4. Energy and water use
    5. Wastes produced
    6. Process emissions
    7. Refrigerants and leakages
    8. Services employed
  2. Emission factor for each of the input type.
    (The amount of greenhouse gases released per unit of input)

Learn from the competitors

Sustainability is a very competitive space, where many innovators are working to replace established manual / off-line tools. So it was essential to understand these competing tools to identify what additional value we could offer.

While researching these tools, it became apparent that each of them works very differently to try to simply the complicated PCF calculation process.

Key variations:

  1. Level of automation:
    1. Aided emission factors (EFs) sourcing: where tools allow users to choose from a pre-loaded database.
    2. Semi automated EFs sourcing: where tools attempt to automatically match user defined material with emission factors.
  2. Anticipated level of knowledge in users:
    1. High:where tools provide highly editable but minimally guided product-object builder.
    2. Low(er):where step-based UI guide user through each data fields.
  3. Quality vs quantity of calculations:
    An effect of differing level of automations means that more PCFs could be calculated by highly automated platforms. However there is a trade of where each level of automation relies on additional value assumptions, and therefore can reduce the level of accuracy of the results.

Learn from the users

The users on M2030 are split by 2 major segments:

  1. Corporate buyers concerned with improving their supply chain. In collaboration with our sales, and sustainability consultations functions, many discussions were carried out with buyers.

    Key learning

    1. Most buyers are at early stage of PCFs journey, and appreciate it would be the same for most suppliers.
    2. Initially they want to have a limited, controlled rollouts for selected suppliers and products. To avoid overwhelming suppliers, and to overtime better understand the benefit of PCFs for themselves.
    3. Data accuracy is important to inform effective business decisions.
  2. Manufacturing suppliers providing their products to buyers. To learn more about supplier’s experience and expectation, an initial survey was carried out.

    Key learning

    1. 78% of respondents have zero to basic understanding of PCFs only.
    2. 86% are willing to share high level PCF results with buyers, and even other suppliers.
    3. Only 4% have already calculated PCFs for most/all their products.
    4. The 2 major reason for not having these PCFs are:
      1. There were no previous request from customers.
      2. Insufficient internal resources.
